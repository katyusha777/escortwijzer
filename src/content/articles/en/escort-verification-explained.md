A "verified" badge on a Dutch escort profile means something different on every platform: on [Intimate](https://intimate.nl/verificatie) a document check is mandatory before any ad goes live, while on Kinky and Sexjobs an advertiser only has to show ID if the site suspects they are underage, according to [Sekswerk.info](https://www.sekswerk.info/en/work/getting-started-as-a-sex-worker/finding-clients-online). So a badge proves identity and age at one moment of checking — it says nothing about whether the photos are accurate.

First, the disclosure: this site is run by the team behind Intimate, and our [methodology](/en/platforms/methodology/) explains how we keep the reviews honest. Which is exactly why Intimate gets measured with the same stick as everyone else below.

## Why does verification even exist here?

Because sex work is a legal, regulated industry in the Netherlands, most of the market runs through public advertising platforms rather than street work or backpage-style grey zones. Legal does not mean scam-free, though. Fake profiles built from stolen photos exist on every large platform, and their business model is almost always the same: get you to pay a "deposit" or "booking fee" before a meeting that will never happen.

Verification badges are the platforms' answer to that problem. The catch, for a visitor who doesn't read Dutch, is that the same word — "geverifieerd" — covers three very different levels of checking.

## What does "verified" check on each platform?

Here is what we could establish per platform, with sources, as of August 2026:

| Platform | What is checked | Mandatory? | What happens to the documents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intimate | Document verification before an ad can be published; badge shown on profile | Yes — a condition for advertising | Processed by an external party "fully in line with GDPR guidelines"; "photos deleted after the check" (own verification page) |
| Kinky.nl | ID check only when the site doubts the advertiser's age (per Sekswerk.info); badge optional | No | Not publicly documented |
| Sexjobs | ID check only on suspicion the advertiser is too young (per Sekswerk.info) | No | Not publicly documented |

Two honest footnotes. Kinky's and Sexjobs' own help pages sat behind a bot check when we tried to read them, so the rows above rest on Sekswerk.info — the information platform of Soa Aids Nederland, a solid third-party source but not first-hand documentation. And Intimate's public page does not list which photos and documents are actually requested; that part sits behind the login. What the page does state, we quote directly: documents are processed by an external party under GDPR, and the photos are deleted after the check.

## Does a badge mean the photos are real?

No, and this is the single most useful thing to understand before you book anything.

A badge tells you that at some point, someone confirmed a real adult was behind the profile. That kills off the crudest category of fraud: invented profiles that exist purely to harvest deposits. It does not tell you the photos are recent, unedited, or even of the same person. Verification is an identity check, not a quality mark — a profile can be genuinely verified and still show photos from eight years ago.

The reverse also holds. On platforms where verification is optional, most profiles are unverified and perfectly real. Read a missing badge as neutral, and a present badge as one extra green flag. Then do the checks that actually catch fakes — reverse image search, comparing ad text across profiles, watching for advance-payment requests — all covered in our guide to [spotting fake profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/).

One rule outranks every badge: anyone who asks you to pay anything before the appointment — gift cards, crypto, a payment link, a "verification fee" for you as a client — is running a scam. In the Netherlands you pay in cash, in person, at the start of the appointment. No legitimate independent or agency deviates from that in a way that involves paying a stranger online first.

## What happens to the ID documents behind the badge?

The question almost nobody asks, and our strongest opinion in this piece.

Every verification starts with a sex worker uploading a copy of their ID to a commercial platform. The Dutch government considers loose ID copies risky enough that it built a dedicated app for them: [KopieID](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/identiteitsfraude/vraag-en-antwoord/veilige-kopie-identiteitsbewijs), published by the national Identity Data Service, which strikes out the citizen service number and photo and watermarks the copy with the recipient and date — because an unmasked ID copy in the wrong hands is ready-made identity fraud.

Our position: verification without a documented deletion policy is a liability, not a feature. A platform that collects and keeps ID copies is building a database that, in one breach, exposes thousands of workers — people for whom a leaked identity means fraud risk plus a very real chance of being outed. The badge you see on the front is paid for with a risk on the back that clients never see.

Held to that standard, Intimate is the only platform in the table that promises deletion after the check on its own page, and that genuinely counts for something. But the promise is not auditable: the external processor is unnamed, the process is not publicly documented, and there is no transparency report. You take it on trust. Kinky and Sexjobs publish, as far as we could find, no policy at all on what happens to an uploaded ID — which is not proof of bad practice, but is exactly the kind of vagueness that shouldn't surround identity documents.

## How should you actually use badges when booking?

As one filter among several, weighted by platform.

On Intimate, every live ad passed a mandatory document check, so the badge carries real weight there — the open question is only photo accuracy. On Kinky or Sexjobs, a badge is a meaningful plus over the unverified majority; no badge means the ordinary checks do the work. Cross-reference the photos and compare prices against the market — our ranking of the [best escort sites in the Netherlands](/en/platforms/best-escort-sites-netherlands/) includes what typical rates look like, and a price far below market is its own warning.

Last habit worth keeping: read any badge as "checked on some date", never as "true today". Identity ages slowly; ad photos age fast.
