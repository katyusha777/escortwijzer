An escort booking in the Netherlands works in four simple steps: you send a polite message with the date, duration and location, you answer any screening questions, you confirm the rate up front, and on the day you pay in cash at the start and behave like a decent guest. Sex work between consenting adults is legal here, so the whole process is closer to booking a professional service than most first-timers expect.

Where first appointments actually go wrong is in the details: arriving late, haggling at the door, or assuming services that were never agreed. This guide walks through the entire process, from first message to goodbye, so your first booking — and every one after — goes smoothly.

## How does booking an escort work, step by step?

A booking follows a fixed sequence: contact, screening, confirmation, appointment. Respect that order and you will almost always be welcomed warmly.

**1. Contact.** Choose an ad on a reputable platform and check that the profile is genuine first — our guide to [spotting fake escort profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/) shows you exactly how. Then send a short, concrete message: who you are (a first name is enough), when you want to meet, for how long, and where — at their place (*incall*) or at yours or your hotel (*outcall*). Skip explicit language in the first message; it reads as disrespectful and gets many enquiries ignored on the spot.

**2. Screening.** Expect questions: your full name, phone number, sometimes your hotel name and room number, or a brief video call. This is not personal distrust — it is the standard safety ritual of a profession built on meeting strangers.

**3. Confirmation.** Pin down the rate, duration and location explicitly in writing. If the ad doesn't state what is and isn't offered, ask politely while booking. Whatever is agreed at this point is the entire deal — nothing more, nothing less.

**4. The appointment.** Be on time. Not twenty minutes early (the previous appointment may still be running), not late (lost minutes come out of your booked time, not on top of it). Ringing the bell five minutes before the agreed time is perfect.

## Why is screening normal — and mutual?

Screening runs in both directions: the escort screens you for safety, and you are equally entitled to verify that the profile is real. Both sides have a legitimate interest.

For the sex worker, screening is the single most important safety measure that exists. They are walking into a room with a total stranger; a real name, a phone number that checks out and a normal conversation beforehand filter out the vast majority of problems. Anyone who refuses screening or jokes their way around it gets declined — rightly so. The honest downside: screening means handing your real name and number to a stranger before you have met. That trade-off has no workaround — and a profile that skips screening entirely is the one to worry about.

For you, the mirror image applies. Check that the photos are consistent, that the profile carries platform verification, and that the phone number isn't attached to ten different ads. Never pay in advance via payment link, gift card or crypto "to confirm the booking" — that is a scam in virtually every case. Legitimate escorts don't ask for deposits through anonymous channels; at most, a small traceable deposit for long or travel bookings.

The Dutch government permits prostitution between consenting adults precisely because a regulated, verifiable market is safer than a hidden one — screening and verification are what separate the legal market from its shady edges.

## What is the money etiquette?

The rule is simple: the agreed amount, in cash, at the start of the appointment, no discussion. Everything after that moment is more relaxed for both of you.

- **The envelope.** Put the money in an unsealed envelope and hand it over when you arrive, or place it somewhere visible. Counting banknotes face to face is awkward for everyone; the envelope signals the business side is settled.
- **Up front, not after.** Paying at the start is the norm in the Netherlands. It takes the subject off the table.
- **Never haggle at the door.** The rate was confirmed when you booked. Offering less on arrival is the classic way to watch the appointment end instantly — and to land on a blacklist shared among colleagues. If a rate feels too high, don't book, or book a shorter session.
- **Exact change.** Bring the precise amount. "Do you have change?" is not a good opening line.

For what a realistic Dutch rate actually looks like — per hour, per city, incall versus outcall — see our [escort price guide](/en/guides/escort-prices-netherlands/). The short version: €150 to €250 per hour is the realistic independent range in 2026, and a rate far below that is usually a fake profile or bait pricing. Some escorts also take a Tikkie or card payment on the spot, but cash remains the Dutch default.

## What hygiene is expected?

Freshly showered, clean clothes, brushed teeth — immediately before the appointment, not that morning. Poor hygiene is the most-cited complaint among sex workers and simultaneously the easiest one to avoid.

At an incall you may almost always shower first — a clean towel will be waiting — and doing so is often expected. Take that shower even if you already had one at home; it's a small ritual that builds trust. For an outcall to your hotel: tidy the room and shower shortly before the agreed time.

Breath counts too. Gum or a toothbrush after coffee, go easy on alcohol beforehand (visibly drunk clients are unpleasant for everyone and are regularly turned away), and if you have a cold or a cold sore, say so honestly and reschedule. Condom use is the standard across the Dutch industry; trying to negotiate around it is not a "request" but a red flag that ends your reputation as a client immediately.

## How do consent and boundaries work?

Everything that happens is either agreed in advance or explicitly offered in the moment — never assumed. The fact that a service appears in someone else's ad, or that you have "paid for the hour", gives you zero claim to anything this particular person doesn't offer.

In practice:

- **Ask beforehand.** If you want something specific, name it politely while booking. You'll know immediately whether it's available, and at what rate.
- **No means no, including halfway through.** An escort can draw a line at any moment, even on something discussed earlier. Consent is continuous, not a one-time signature.
- **No "surprises".** Secretly removing a condom (known as *stealthing*) is a criminal sexual offence in the Netherlands. So is filming or photographing anyone without explicit permission.
- **It goes both ways.** You are allowed boundaries too. A good escort will ask what you enjoy; you are just as free to decline anything.

Safety here runs in both directions: the same habits that protect the sex worker — screening, services confirmed in advance, payment without friction — protect you against misunderstandings, scams and conflict. An appointment where both people feel safe is also, demonstrably, a better one.

## What is the etiquette for cancelling?

Cancel as early as you can, always with a message, never by simply not turning up. The *no-show* is the cardinal sin of this industry: the reserved time has become unsellable, and your number goes straight onto a blacklist — one that colleagues share with each other.

Reasonable practice:

- **Well in advance (24+ hours):** a short, friendly message is enough. Offer to reschedule.
- **Same day:** offer partial compensation, especially if costs were already incurred (travel, a hotel room, childcare). Many independent escorts publish cancellation terms right on their profile — read them before you book.
- **When the escort cancels on you:** it happens — illness, screening that didn't check out, personal circumstances. React the way you'd want to be treated; clients who handle it like adults go to the front of the queue next time.

## How do you stay discreet — in both directions?

Discretion is a mutual contract: you expect your details to stay private, and exactly the same applies to the person opposite you. Use a separate or neutral communication channel if that makes you comfortable, but don't play games with fake names during screening — verified and discreet can coexist just fine.

What you never do: share a sex worker's real name, face or personal details, not even "anonymously" on a forum. No unsolicited photos. No chatting outside the context of a booking ("hey, how was your weekend?") — this is a professional relationship, not a dating app. And if you ever cross paths in public: you don't know each other, unless they greet you first.

## What makes you a good regular client?

A good client is predictable in the best sense: books properly, pays without friction, shows up clean, respects boundaries and leaves on time. Do that consistently and doors open — lighter screening, more flexible hours, and the kind of relaxed appointments both sides look forward to.

The ideal regular's checklist:

1. **Books ahead** instead of "are you free in 20 minutes?"
2. **Pays the full rate** without ever grumbling — and rounds up after an excellent experience.
3. **Respects the clock.** The appointment ends at the agreed moment; overstaying is the subtlest way to stop being welcome.
4. **Leaves an honest review** where the platform supports it, without explicit detail or identifying information.
5. **Treats sex work as work.** No saviour complex, no lovestruck messages, no asking for a discount "now that we know each other".

If you follow these customs, you have already understood the most important thing: an escort appointment is a service between two adults who treat each other with respect. Everything else — the ease, the fun, the reason you'd book again — follows naturally from that.
