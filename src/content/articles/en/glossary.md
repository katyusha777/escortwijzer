This glossary explains the 33 terms and abbreviations you will meet on Dutch escort sites, in ads and in municipal rules — from *privéontvangst* to APV. Each definition is short and factual, and where the law matters we name it, because that is where foreign visitors are misled most often: no, Dutch sex work is not "tolerated" — it has been fully legal since 2000.

## Forms of sex work

**Escort** — A sex worker who visits the client by appointment (home or hotel) or receives the client at her own location, booked through a licensed agency or directly via an advertising platform. The most discreet segment of the Dutch market and the one most visitors use.

**Privéontvangst** — Literally "private reception": an independent sex worker receiving clients at a private address, a home or a rented workspace, with no club or agency involved. The standard ad term for independent incall.

**Thuisontvangst** — Privéontvangst specifically at the worker's own home. Whether this is allowed depends on the municipality: Rotterdam permits it under strict conditions, many other cities ban it.

**Raamprostitutie** — Window prostitution: sex work from a rented window in a licensed window brothel, as in Amsterdam's red light district or Utrecht's Zandpad. You negotiate on the spot, at the window. How windows compare with escorts is covered in [escort vs brothel vs windows](/en/law/escort-brothel-windows-difference/).

**Privéhuis** — A licensed "private house" where several sex workers work and clients walk in without an appointment; you choose on site and pay per unit of time. Less formal than a club, more organised than privéontvangst.

**Club** — A licensed sex club with a bar, where contact starts with a drink and a conversation. Pricier than a privéhuis, because the setting is part of what you pay for.

**Massagesalon (+)** — A massage parlour that also offers erotic services, flagged in ads with a plus sign or "massage with extras". This is only legal with a municipal sex-business licence; parlours offering it without one operate illegally.

**Tippelzone** — A municipally designated zone where street prostitution is permitted within set hours. Most Dutch cities have closed theirs; street prostitution outside a tippelzone is illegal, which is why street offers to tourists are a red flag.

**De Wallen** — Amsterdam's famous window district around the Oudezijds Achterburgwal, the one tourists know. It is a separate world from the escort market, with its own rules and prices — see our [Amsterdam escort guide](/en/cities/escort-amsterdam/) and the [Amsterdam tourist guide](/en/cities/amsterdam-tourist-guide/).

## Ad language and abbreviations

Dutch ads are compact and full of jargon. One rule before the list: every abbreviation describes what someone *offers*, never what a client is entitled to. Everything is agreed in advance.

**Incall / outcall** — Incall: you go to the escort's location; this is the base rate. Outcall: the escort comes to your home or hotel, usually adding €30 to €80 for travel time. Tourists almost always book outcall.

**High class** — The premium segment, roughly €250 to €400 per hour and up, often via agencies with screening and longer bookings such as dinner dates. The label is not protected; the rate tells you more than the words. What is normal per segment is in [escort prices in the Netherlands](/en/guides/escort-prices-netherlands/).

**GFE** — Girlfriend experience: an appointment that feels like a date, with attention, conversation and intimacy rather than a purely transactional meeting.

**PSE** — Pornstar experience: service modelled on pornography — more explicit and more intense in tone and pace than GFE. What it involves differs per person and is discussed beforehand.

**OWO** — "Oral without": oral sex without a condom. A plain ad term stating that someone offers this; it carries a higher STI risk than protected oral, and the choice always sits with the worker.

**CIM** — "Come in mouth". Like OWO, a statement of what someone does or does not offer — never a default part of an appointment.

**Verificatie / geverifieerd** — Dutch for verification / verified: the badge showing a platform confirmed the advertiser is real, usually via ID or a verification photo; since 1 April 2026 an identity check on new ads is legally required. What each platform actually checks varies a lot — we tested it in [verification explained](/en/guides/escort-verification-explained/). Our position: verification without a stated deletion policy for those ID scans is a half-measure, because a database of passports is a risk in itself.

**Screening** — The pre-booking check, and it runs both ways: the escort asks your name, number or hotel details, and you check that the profile is genuine. Standard, mutual, and a good sign; how to handle it is in the [first-time etiquette guide](/en/guides/escort-etiquette-first-time/).

**Lokprijs** — Dutch for bait price: an advertised rate (say €80 per hour) that turns out to be fiction on arrival, with everything "extra" and the real cost €250. Below €100 per hour a legal independent can barely operate — how to spot these ads is in [spotting fake escort profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/).

## Law and licensing

**Opheffing bordeelverbod** — The lifting of the brothel ban on 1 October 2000, which took the running of sex businesses out of Dutch criminal law. Brothels, clubs and escort agencies have been legal, licensed businesses ever since; the full legal picture is in [is escort legal in the Netherlands?](/en/law/is-escort-legal-netherlands/)

**Gedoogbeleid** — "Tolerance policy": formally illegal but not prosecuted under conditions — the coffeeshop regime. Sex work has *not* fallen under it since 2000: it is simply legal. English-language media reuse the "tolerated" frame constantly, and it is wrong.

**APV** — Algemene Plaatselijke Verordening, the municipal by-laws where each city's sex-work policy lives: how many sex businesses may exist, where, and under which conditions. This is why the rules differ from city to city.

**Escortvergunning** — The operating licence an escort agency needs from its municipality; The Hague, for instance, issues it one year at a time (fee € 1,704). An honest caveat: there is no public national register of licensed agencies, so as a client you can rarely verify a licence yourself — a real weakness of the system.

**Nuloptie** — The "zero option": a municipality's policy of issuing no sex-business licences at all. Part of the Dutch municipalities use it, which is why some towns have no legal venues.

**Wrp / WRS** — Two successive bills for a national licensing system: the Wet regulering prostitutie (Wrp) and its successor the Wet regulering sekswerk (WRS, bill 35.715). Neither became law; the cabinet formally dropped the WRS in April 2026.

**Wgts** — Wet gemeentelijk toezicht seksbedrijven: the announced follow-up to the WRS, giving municipalities a firmer legal basis for supervision. As of mid-2026 it is not yet law.

**Bibob** — An integrity-screening law that lets municipalities vet sex-business operators for criminal ties before and during a licence.

**Exploitant** — The licence holder running a sex business — club, window brothel or escort agency — with a legal duty of care for the workers. The national age floor for everyone: 18, and 21 in many cities — details in [legal age and client risk](/en/law/escort-legal-age-client-risk/).

## Money and booking

**KvK** — Kamer van Koophandel, the Dutch Chamber of Commerce. Independent sex workers register as entrepreneurs (one-time fee € 85.15); the tax office is informed automatically.

**Zzp** — Zelfstandige zonder personeel, "self-employed without staff": the business form of most independent escorts, with quarterly VAT returns and annual income tax.

**Opting-in** — A Dutch tax arrangement for workers at a club or agency who do not want to be entrepreneurs: the operator withholds payroll tax and remits it to the tax office.

**KOR** — The small-business VAT exemption: below € 20,000 turnover per year, no VAT needs to be charged. Relevant for part-time workers.

**Tikkie / PIN** — Dutch payment habits you will encounter: Tikkie is a bank-linked payment-request app, PIN means paying by debit card. Both exist in this industry, but the norm remains cash, handed over at the start of the appointment.

**Aanbetaling** — Deposit. With independent escorts, a demand for one is almost always a scam marker: anyone requiring payment before the appointment via paysafecard, gift cards or crypto is a fraudster. Only established licensed agencies sometimes legitimately ask a traceable deposit for long bookings.
