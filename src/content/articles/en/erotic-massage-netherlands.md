An erotic massage is a massage with an erotic element, given in a parlour or by appointment — and legally its own form of sex business, not the same as an escort. A simple erotic or body-to-body massage often costs €80 to €150 per hour; tantra sessions run up to around €295 for two hours.

Two things are worth knowing before you book: it is a separate licensed category with its own legal line, and the price is usually lower than an escort's. Here is what a massage parlour actually is, where the legal line runs, what the standard terms mean, and how to tell a legitimate parlour from a front.

## What is an erotic massage parlour — and how does it differ from an escort?

An erotic massage parlour is a fixed premises where a masseur or masseuse gives massages with an erotic element. That is a different working form from an escort or privéontvangst, and the market treats it that way.

The difference comes down to who travels and what you are buying:

- **Escort** — the sex worker comes to you (hotel or home).
- **Privéontvangst** — you go to the sex worker's private address.
- **Massage parlour** — you go to a licensed parlour where the service is massage, not an escort-style appointment.

The erotic massage parlour has its own place in the law: it is one of the five forms of sex business a municipality requires an operating licence for, alongside brothels, escort businesses, window businesses and sex cinemas ([Ondernemersplein](https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/wetten-en-regels/vergunning-seksbedrijf/)). How the other forms relate to each other is covered in [escort vs brothel vs windows](/en/law/escort-brothel-windows-difference/).

## Where does the legal line run?

This is the heart of it, and it is subtler than most ads suggest. An erotic massage comes in two legal flavours, depending on the municipality: licensed and unlicensed ([Sekswerkerfgoed](https://sekswerkerfgoed.nl/vormen-van-prostitutie-een-overzicht/)).

A parlour offering massage with a sexual element needs a sex-business licence — the same operating licence as a club or escort business. A parlour giving only therapeutic, non-sexual massage does not; that is simply a massage practice.

The problem is the grey zone between them. An ad for "massage with extras" or "massage +" with no licence is operating illegally. The label itself says nothing about legality; the licence does. And that is the honest limitation of this topic: there is no public per-parlour register you can check, so from an ad alone you often cannot tell whether a parlour is licensed, works therapeutically, or is simply using "massage" as bait.

## What do tantra, nuru, body-to-body and "happy ending" mean?

The terms get used loosely, but they stand for different things. Factually, without the packaging:

**Body-to-body** — the masseur uses the whole body for contact, not just the hands.

**Nuru** — a full-body massage that originated in Yokohama, Japan in the 1990s, using a slippery, water-soluble gel that allows extensive skin-to-skin contact. The gel was originally made from seaweed high in fucoidan; modern versions usually use sodium polyacrylate ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuru_(massage))).

**Tantra** — a slower, ritualised style built around breath and whole-body sensation rather than an endpoint. A tantra massage therefore often runs longer than an ordinary session.

**Happy ending** — colloquial for a massage that ends in manual stimulation. Not a legal term, and whether it is offered legally again depends on that licence. For more ad terminology and what it means in practice, see [escort services explained](/en/guides/escort-services-explained/).

## What does an erotic massage cost?

Lower than an escort, in most cases — that is the honest summary. Advertised ranges as of mid-2026:

- **Simple erotic or body-to-body massage:** often around €80 to €150 per hour.
- **Tantra massage:** roughly €155 for an hour, €225 for 90 minutes, €295 for two hours.
- **Duo or four-hands massage:** €300 to €400 per hour.

For comparison: an escort starts at €150 to €250 per hour with an independent. A short or simple massage sits below that, while a long tantra session can match or exceed escort level — the "cheaper" flag holds mainly at the bottom end, not automatically across the board. ## How do you tell a legitimate parlour from a front?

The signals are the same as for any regulated service, and the licence is the pivot.

A legitimate parlour has a fixed location, a real business name, published prices, and a consistent account of what a session involves. An unlicensed front does the opposite: shifting addresses, no clear prices, "massage +" with no further explanation, and pressure to pay in advance. That last one is the clearest alarm — as with escorts, any payment before the appointment via gift cards or crypto is a scam. How to tell those ads apart is in [spotting fake escort profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/), and what a verification badge on a platform is worth in [escort verification explained](/en/guides/escort-verification-explained/).

Our opinion: for someone who wants a lower-commitment, non-penetrative experience, a licensed massage parlour is a legitimate and often cheaper alternative to an escort — but only a licensed one. The reason is simple: the licence is the one checkable signal separating a regulated business from a front, and the unlicensed "massage +" ad is exactly where both the scams and the legally grey operations live.

Booking itself is legal, in all forms, as long as the sex worker is 18 or older and works voluntarily. The choice between a massage and an escort is not a legal one but a practical one: what you want, what you want to pay, and whether the parlour holds the licence that makes the service legal.
