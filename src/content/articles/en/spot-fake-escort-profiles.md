The fastest way to expose a fake escort profile: **reverse-search the photos, sanity-check the rate against the market, and refuse any payment before the appointment.** A genuine profile passes all three checks; a fake one almost always fails at least one.

Escort scams are almost never about the appointment — it never happens. The goal is your money (deposits, "verification fees") or your fear (blackmail). Once you know the standard scripts, you can see through them in minutes.

## What are the most common escort scams?

**Four scams account for nearly the entire problem: stolen photos, the deposit trick, bait-and-switch, and blackmail.** Here is how each works:

- **Stolen photos / fully fake profiles.** Photos lifted from models, OnlyFans accounts or escorts abroad, pasted onto an invented name with a Dutch-sounding story. The goal is to pull you onto WhatsApp and talk you into a payment. Details often give it away: professional studio shots paired with a rock-bottom rate, or an "escort in Utrecht" with palm trees in the background.
- **The deposit or voucher trick.** The "escort" wants money before meeting: a reservation fee, verification, driver costs, a security deposit. Always via paysafecard, gift cards or crypto — anonymous and irreversible. After you pay comes either silence or a follow-up demand ("the driver is outside, €50 more as deposit"). This is the most-reported variant at the Dutch national fraud desk, [Fraudehelpdesk](https://www.fraudehelpdesk.nl/fraude-melden/).
- **Bait-and-switch.** The appointment does happen, but the person who opens the door is not the person in the photos, or the advertised rate doubles once you arrive. Expect social pressure to stay anyway. The correct response is to turn around and leave — you owe nothing.
- **Blackmail (sextortion).** After explicit chats or a video call comes the threat: pay, or screenshots go to your employer or family. Sometimes a second scammer poses as "police" or as the "angry father of a minor" — a well-known script designed to cause panic. Never pay: anyone who pays once lands on a list and gets targeted again.

## How do you verify an escort ad step by step?

**Five checks, about ten minutes in total, filter out virtually every fake profile.** Run them before you agree to anything:

1. **Reverse-search the photos.** Google Lens or TinEye: right-click the image or upload a screenshot. If the same photos surface under other names, in other countries, or on stock-photo and model sites, you are done — walk away.
2. **Sanity-check the rate.** An independent escort in the Netherlands realistically charges €150 to €250 per hour; far below that, operating legally does not add up. A €70-per-hour ad with glossy photos is suspicious by definition. Current per-city ranges are in our [escort price guide](/en/guides/escort-prices-netherlands/).
3. **Check the platform's verification status.** A badge is not a guarantee, but the combination of a verified profile, a market-standard rate and consistent photos is strong. What a badge is worth on each platform is covered below.
4. **Watch the phone and WhatsApp behaviour.** Red flags: a foreign number on a "Rotterdam escort", refusing even a short call, steering to payment within three messages, copy-pasted text blocks in broken Dutch or English, and artificial urgency ("only today, book now"). A real escort will take a brief call or send a voice note; a scammer never will.
5. **Search the phone number and name.** Paste the Dutch mobile number (06-number) into Google. If it appears on multiple ads under different names and cities, you are looking at a scam network.

Consistency is the key word: name, age, city, rate and photos should match across every platform. Scammers run dozens of profiles at once, and that is exactly where they slip.

One important nuance: **screening by the escort herself is normal and actually a good sign.** An independent escort who asks for your name, your hotel and room number for an outcall, or who wants a short phone call before confirming, is doing safety work — for her and for you. The difference from a scammer is simple: screening costs you information and a few minutes, never money. The moment "verification" requires a payment, you are dealing with a scammer.

## How does verification actually work on Dutch escort platforms?

**Not every checkmark means the same thing — verification depth varies widely between platforms.** Broadly, the Dutch market runs on two models:

- **Full profile verification.** The platform checks every profile before publication against an identity document plus proof (such as a dated verification photo), establishing that the person in the photos exists and personally controls the ad. Intimate works this way, verifying every profile with ID and proof before it goes live — full transparency: Intimate is our sister platform, and our [Intimate review](/en/platforms/intimate-review/) describes how that check works in practice.
- **Ad moderation.** Established players such as kinky.nl and the classic classifieds sites screen ads at submission for rule violations and signs of abuse, but do not verify every profile against ID. That is not negligence — with tens of thousands of ads, full verification is a massive operation — and as the market leader, kinky.nl also hosts the largest number of genuine profiles. It simply means you, the client, need to run the checks above yourself. Our [kinky.nl review](/en/platforms/kinky-review/) covers their approach in detail.

The practical rule: **a badge does not replace your own checks, but a profile with no verification at all deserves extra suspicion.** Which platforms use which model is laid out in our overview of the [best escort sites in the Netherlands](/en/platforms/best-escort-sites-netherlands/).

## What should you do if you have been scammed?

**Stop all contact, preserve the evidence, then report — in that order.** Concretely:

1. **Do not block anything until you have saved the evidence.** Screenshot the profile, the chats, the phone number, and every payment request and receipt. Scam profiles disappear fast.
2. **Report it to Fraudehelpdesk** at [fraudehelpdesk.nl](https://www.fraudehelpdesk.nl/fraude-melden/) — the Dutch national fraud desk. They log the scam network, warn others, and point you to the right follow-up channel. They handle reports in English too.
3. **File a police report** via [politie.nl](https://www.politie.nl/aangifte-of-melding-doen) or by calling 0900-8844. Internet fraud can be reported online; when five or more reports name the same scammer, banks are notified as well. And to repeat: booking an escort is legal in the Netherlands — there is nothing to be embarrassed about when filing.
4. **Paid by bank transfer or payment link?** Call your bank immediately; sometimes a transfer can still be stopped or the receiving account frozen. With paysafecard or gift cards, the money is unfortunately almost always gone.
5. **Being blackmailed?** Do not pay, stop responding, and file a report. [Slachtofferhulp Nederland](https://www.slachtofferhulp.nl/gebeurtenissen/oplichting-en-fraude/online-handel/aangifte-doen/) (the Dutch victim support organisation) offers free help with the next steps.

## Can you avoid escort scams entirely?

**Almost — if you never pay in advance and only book after the five checks, you are of no interest to scammers.** The entire scam economy runs on haste and shame: haste to rush you past the checks, shame to keep you from reporting. Take the ten minutes, book through a platform that seriously moderates or verifies, and pay cash at the appointment itself.
