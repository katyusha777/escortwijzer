Hiring an escort in Amsterdam is fully legal: sex work between consenting adults is permitted throughout the Netherlands, and Amsterdam regulates the industry through municipal licensing — escort agencies need an operating permit from the city, just like the famous window brothels. As a visitor, booking a licensed agency escort or a verified independent to your hotel is a lawful, well-trodden path; the illegal edges are street solicitation, unlicensed middlemen and anyone under 21.

Amsterdam is also the most heavily touristed paid-sex market in Europe, which means it has the country's highest prices and its highest concentration of scams aimed at visitors. This guide covers how the scene actually works in 2026, what things cost, how hotel visits work, and the handful of rules — legal and unwritten — that keep you out of trouble.

## Are escorts legal in Amsterdam?

Yes, under a clear licensing regime. The Netherlands lifted its brothel ban in 2000, making the operation of sex businesses legal nationwide, and Amsterdam sets its local rules in the APV (*Algemene Plaatselijke Verordening*, the city's general municipal by-law): running a prostitution business requires a licence under article 3.27, and escort agencies need their own operating licence under article 3.40. An agency without a municipal permit is operating illegally.

Three things make Amsterdam stricter than the national baseline:

- **Minimum age 21.** Nationally, sex workers must be at least 18, but Amsterdam has required a minimum age of 21 since July 2013, a measure aimed at combating trafficking and exploitation. The Dutch government has moved to make 21 the national minimum too.
- **Street prostitution is banned.** The APV prohibits soliciting clients on or from the street (article 2.12), and Amsterdam has no tolerated street-walking zone. Anyone offering you sex on the street is by definition operating illegally — and in practice is almost always the front end of a scam.
- **Window prostitution only in designated zones.** Window brothels are confined to three licensed areas: De Wallen (the Red Light District), the Singelgebied and the Ruysdaelkade.

## How is paid sex organised in Amsterdam?

The market has three segments — windows, clubs, and escorts — and despite the fame of the first, the third is how most visitors actually arrange things. Escort visits to hotels are discreet, conducted in English, and bookable from your phone.

**Windows.** The city's iconic format: workers independently rent a window room from a licensed operator and set their own prices, services and clientele. Transparent and direct, but the opposite of discreet — you're negotiating through a door on one of the busiest streets in Europe.

**Clubs and privéhuizen.** Licensed venues ("private houses") where you meet on site. There are relatively few inside the city proper; part of the club scene sits in surrounding municipalities.

**Escorts (incall and outcall).** An appointment at the escort's place (*incall*) or at your hotel (*outcall*), booked through a licensed agency or directly with an independent via an advertising platform. This is the fastest-growing and most discreet segment — and the one where verifying who you're talking to matters most, since all you see beforehand is a profile. ## Can an escort visit my hotel?

Usually yes — hotel outcalls are the standard visitor booking, and most Amsterdam hotels allow registered guests to receive visitors in their rooms. Escorts who work outcall visit hotels every working day and know how to arrive looking like any other guest; discretion is part of the service.

Two practical caveats. First, some hostels, budget chains and canal-house B&Bs enforce strict no-visitor policies, sometimes requiring visitors to register at reception or banning non-guests after a set hour — check the house rules before booking an outcall, not after. Second, expect the screening to run in both directions: a professional will ask for your hotel name, room number and the name your booking is under. That's normal and mutual — it protects them walking into a stranger's room, exactly as platform verification protects you. If a hotel visit is impossible, most independents offer incall at private apartments, typically outside the centre in districts like Zuid or West.

## How much does an escort cost in Amsterdam?

Amsterdam is the most expensive city in the Netherlands for paid companionship — budget above the national average in every segment. High rents, relentless tourist demand and a large high-end independent scene all push rates up.

Roughly by segment:

- **Windows:** the classic short visit, with commonly cited starting rates around €50 for 15–20 minutes; anything longer or extra is negotiated per window, before you step inside.
- **Clubs/privéhuizen:** an entry fee plus time-based rates; total spend usually lands between window and escort level.
- **Escorts:** priced per hour. Amsterdam independents and agencies charge noticeably more than the same service costs in Groningen or Maastricht; outcall to your hotel costs more than incall (travel time), and multi-hour or overnight bookings get cheaper per hour.

One Amsterdam-specific pricing rule to burn into memory: an ad quoting far below the market is almost certainly fake or bait pricing. This city's cost structure makes genuine bargain rates impossible, and "too cheap" is the single most reliable scam signal there is.

## What happened to the "erotic centre"?

The plan is dead — scrapped in 2026. Since 2019, Mayor Femke Halsema had pushed to move window prostitution out of the medieval centre into one large purpose-built "erotic centre", intended to cut nuisance in De Wallen. In December 2025 the city concluded a site on Europaboulevard in Amsterdam-Zuid was financially and spatially feasible, but punted the final decision until after the March 2026 municipal elections.

The delay became a cancellation. The project had faced years of resistance: roughly 800 formal public objections, street protests, an opposed district council in Zuid — and, notably, opposition from sex workers themselves, many of whom argued the visibility of De Wallen is precisely what keeps them safe. When the new coalition of PRO Amsterdam and D66 presented its agreement at the end of May 2026, the erotic centre was struck from the books; the parties want to explore several small-scale locations instead, though the mayor has already noted the city lacks the enforcement staff to run them.

What this means for a visitor is simple: De Wallen isn't going anywhere. The windows, the licensing regime and the booking landscape described in this guide are the reality for the coming years.

## Which neighbourhoods should you know?

Three zones host licensed windows; escorts, by nature, work city-wide. Each zone has its own character:

- **De Wallen (Red Light District).** The oldest and largest window area — and a living neighbourhood of around 9,000 residents layered with bars, coffeeshops and galleries. It is heavily policed at night, and the city has spent recent years tightening rules on crowds, drinking on the street and tour groups.
- **Singelgebied.** A smaller cluster of windows near the Singel canal by Nieuwendijk. Same licensing regime, a fraction of the crowds.
- **Ruysdaelkade (De Pijp).** The "local" strip along the canal in De Pijp, outside the tourist flow. No party circus around it; the clientele is more Amsterdam than abroad.

For escort bookings, geography barely matters: outcalls cover every hotel in the city plus the Schiphol airport hotels (usually with a travel surcharge), and incall apartments cluster in residential districts you'd otherwise never visit.

## How do you avoid scams as a visitor?

Book only through verified profiles on reputable platforms or through licensed agencies — and treat every offer that comes to you on the street or via a taxi driver as a scam, because it is. Amsterdam's tourist volume sustains an entire economy of tricks aimed at people who don't know the market. The classics:

1. **The taxi "recommendation".** A driver knows "a great club, very good price". The destination is an overpriced clip joint paying him commission, and the bill at the end bears no relation to anything you agreed. Decline, every time.
2. **Street solicitation.** Illegal under the APV (article 2.12) and almost always the opening move of a robbery, a drug hustle or a fake-room scam. Nobody legitimate sells sex on Amsterdam's streets.
3. **The advance-payment ad.** A profile asks for a deposit by payment link, gift card or crypto "to confirm the booking", then vanishes. Legitimate escorts in the Netherlands are paid in cash at the start of the appointment — remote prepayment to someone you've never met is the scam, full stop. Our guide to [spotting fake escort profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/) walks through every verification check before you send a single message.
4. **The too-good price.** See the pricing section: in this city, a steep discount *is* the red flag.

English, incidentally, is no barrier anywhere in this market — workers, agencies and platforms all operate in it daily, so never let anyone position themselves as your necessary "translator" or middleman.

## What are the unwritten rules?

One rule towers over all others: **never photograph the window workers.** It is the most strictly enforced norm in De Wallen — not a law you'll find printed, but a boundary defended instantly by the workers, their minders and even residents. Cameras pointed at windows get blocked, phones get grabbed, and the stories of phones ending up in the canal are not urban legend. Shoot the canals and the architecture; never the people behind the glass.

Beyond that, remember De Wallen is a residential neighbourhood, and behave like it: no shouting, no gawking in packs pressed against windows, no drunken group antics — the city fines street drinking and disorderly behaviour in the centre. At a window or on a booking, standard professional etiquette applies: services and price agreed before anything starts, payment up front, and no is no — services are agreed, never assumed.

Amsterdam runs the most visible, most regulated sex-work landscape in the world. Use the legal channels, verify before you book, respect the people doing the work, and the city's famous infrastructure does exactly what it was designed for: a safe, transparent market between consenting adults.
