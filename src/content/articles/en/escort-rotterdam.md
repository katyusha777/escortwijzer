Hiring an escort in Rotterdam is fully legal: sex work between consenting adults is permitted throughout the Netherlands, and Rotterdam licenses escort agencies under chapter 3 of its municipal by-law, the APV. What surprises most visitors is what the city *doesn't* have: no red light district, no windows, no street zone — the last windows in Katendrecht vanished in the early 1990s and the tolerated street-walking zone on Keileweg closed in September 2005.

So if you searched "Rotterdam red light district" hoping for a Dutch postcard scene, the honest answer is that it does not exist. What Rotterdam has instead is a large, entirely appointment-based market — escorts visiting hotels, independents receiving at private addresses, a handful of licensed clubs — at the lowest prices of the four big Dutch cities. This guide explains how it works.

## Is escort legal in Rotterdam?

Yes, under municipal licensing. The Netherlands legalised the operation of sex businesses in 2000, and Rotterdam's APV (*Algemene Plaatselijke Verordening*, the general municipal by-law) defines four licence categories for them. Escort agencies are explicitly one: the by-law describes an *escortbedrijf* as mediation between client and sex worker. An agency operating without the municipal permit is breaking the law — the client booking through it generally is not, but you'd be funding the illegal end of a legal market.

Two local rules worth knowing:

- **Minimum age 21.** Licensed Rotterdam businesses may only work with sex workers aged 21 or over — stricter than the national minimum of 18, and a rule Rotterdam shares with Amsterdam and The Hague.
- **Solo home work is legal without a licence.** An independent sex worker may receive clients in their own home without any permit, provided they work alone, are registered at that address and use a single workroom. This is why Dutch ads mention *privéontvangst* — "private reception" at the worker's own place — and why such an ad can be perfectly legal with no agency involved.

## Why doesn't Rotterdam have a red light district?

Because the city dismantled it, earlier and more thoroughly than anywhere else in the country. Katendrecht, the old harbour quarter, was once nationally famous as Rotterdam's answer to Amsterdam's De Wallen — sailors' bars, brothels and windows. Urban renewal squeezed that scene out through the 1980s; by the early 1990s the windows were gone, and today Katendrecht is a gentrified residential peninsula with restaurants where the brothels stood.

The last visible remnant was the Keileweg, a tolerated street-prostitution zone in the western port area that opened in November 1994 and closed in September 2005 after years of neighbourhood complaints and municipal costs running into millions per year. Nothing replaced it.

The practical consequence for a visitor: anyone offering you paid sex on a Rotterdam street is operating illegally, and the entire legitimate market lives online, on advertising platforms and agency sites.

## How do you actually book in Rotterdam?

Three formats, all arranged in advance from your phone — walking in somewhere is not how this city works.

**Outcall escort.** The escort comes to your hotel or apartment. Booked through a licensed agency or directly with an independent on an advertising platform. This is the standard visitor booking, conducted in English without fuss; the Dutch escort scene works in English every day.

**Incall / privéontvangst.** You go to the escort — either a working apartment or, thanks to Rotterdam's solo home-work rule, their own home. Usually the cheapest option because no agency margin and no travel time are priced in. The differences between escorts, private reception and windows are laid out in our [guide to the Dutch formats](/en/law/escort-brothel-windows-difference/).

**Clubs and privéhuizen.** Licensed venues ("private houses") where you meet on site. Rotterdam has a few; the city publishes no count of them.

Because there are no windows, the online ad market *is* the whole market — and that cuts both ways. Every fake profile in the Netherlands can claim to be in Rotterdam, and there is no physical place to see who actually works there. ## How much does an escort cost in Rotterdam?

Expect €140 to €220 per hour for an independent escort — Rotterdam has the sharpest rates of the four big Dutch cities. The supply is the country's largest after Amsterdam, but the tourist premium that pushes Amsterdam rates €50 to €100 per hour higher simply doesn't exist here. Agencies start around €250 per hour, with screening and mediation built into the price.

Add a €30 to €80 outcall surcharge for a hotel visit, standard nationwide. Longer bookings get cheaper per hour — two hours typically runs around 1.7 times the hourly rate, not double. The full breakdown, including overnight rates and how tipping works (short version: it's not expected in the Netherlands), is in our [Dutch escort price guide](/en/guides/escort-prices-netherlands/).

One number to remember: below €100 per hour, an ad is almost never real. Dutch taxes, advertising and workspace costs make legal work at that rate impossible, so a bargain-priced "Rotterdam" profile is bait pricing or a deposit scam.

## Can an escort visit my hotel in Rotterdam?

Almost always. Rotterdam is a conference and port-business city — from the business towers on the Maas to the chains around Centraal Station, hotels here are used to guests receiving visitors, and outcall escorts arrive looking like any other guest. Discretion is part of the product.

Check the house rules first at hostels and budget hotels, some of which require visitors to register at reception or bar non-guests late at night. And expect screening in both directions: a professional will ask your hotel name, room number and the name on the booking before confirming. Answer it plainly — it protects them walking into a stranger's room exactly as platform verification protects you. Someone who asks nothing at all is the worse sign.

Payment is cash at the start of the appointment, in euros; some escorts take a card or Dutch payment app on the spot. What nobody legitimate does is demand prepayment by payment link, gift card or crypto before you've met — that is the single most common escort scam aimed at foreigners, and our guide to [spotting fake escort profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/) shows how to filter it out before you send a message.

## Is Rotterdam better than Amsterdam for this?

For actually booking an appointment: yes, and it isn't close. That's an opinion, so here is the reasoning. Amsterdam's tourist volume sustains an entire scam economy aimed at visitors — street hustlers, taxi commissions, bait-priced ads — and its rates carry a €50–€100 hourly premium for the privilege. Rotterdam has none of the spectacle and therefore none of the predators who feed on it: no street offers to dodge, sharper prices, and the same legal protections and platform infrastructure as everywhere else in the country.

The honest trade-off: Rotterdam gives you nothing to see. No windows, no district to wander through, no way to verify anything with your own eyes — you depend entirely on platform verification and the escort's online footprint, and the city keeps no public register of licensed agencies you could check against. If part of the appeal is the spectacle itself, Amsterdam remains the only show in the country; our [Amsterdam guide](/en/cities/escort-amsterdam/) covers that market, tourist traps included.

For everyone else — the visitor in a hotel by the Maas who wants a discreet, legal, fairly priced appointment — Rotterdam is quietly the best-functioning escort market in the Netherlands.
