Redlights.nl calls itself "the largest prostitution marketplace in the Netherlands" — a claim nobody can verify — but what we could measure adds up to a 6.1 overall, and the standout number is a 7 for moderation: alone among the free Dutch marketplaces, Redlights demonstrably reviews every ad before it goes live, within an hour during office hours. The weak spot is the naming: the "geverifieerd" (verified) label checks photos, not identity.

**Disclosure first:** best-escorts.nl is made by the team behind Intimate.nl, a competitor of Redlights. That's exactly why this review follows our [published methodology](/en/platforms/methodology/) with a verifiable score per criterion — and why Redlights gets full credit where it earns it, because on moderation documentation it beats both Kinky and Sexjobs.

## What is Redlights.nl?

Redlights is a classifieds marketplace for the erotic sector in the Netherlands and Flanders, with a French-language sister portal, Quartier-Rouge.be, for the rest of Belgium. Categories run wider than escorts alone: private reception ("thuisontvangst"), escort, massage, virtual sex and webcam, job listings and business addresses, each split into women, gigolos, shemales, fetish and couples.

The model is standard for this market: advertisers post ads, visitors contact them directly, the platform never brokers anything. Advertising is free — the help centre's own words are "volledig GRATIS" — with one free ad per day, multiple refreshes, and paid premium formulas for better placement.

That free-entry model is shared with Kinky and Sexjobs, and it carries the same consequence everywhere: when posting costs nothing and proves nothing, fake ads get in too. What sets Redlights apart is what happens after posting — and how openly the platform writes about it.

## How good is the verification?

A 5, and the naming is the problem. Redlights has a green "geverifieerd" label and a category-page filter for verified-only ads, which reads like identity control. The help centre is more honest: the label is a "quality label" confirming that photos and videos genuinely belong to the advertiser, granted after a verification photo — a handwritten note referencing Redlights in frame — or a live cam check via Skype.

That's genuinely useful, because stolen photo sets are the raw material of nearly every fake ad. But it is not an ID check: according to Sekswerk.info, the Dutch public-information site for sex workers, Redlights — like Kinky and Sexjobs — only asks for identity documents when it doubts an advertiser is old enough. The label is also voluntary, so an unlabelled ad may be perfectly real, and a photo check says nothing about who actually opens the door. Our [guide to what "verified" means per platform](/en/guides/escort-verification-explained/) covers the differences in detail.

## How is the mobile experience?

Adequate: a 6. The site scales properly on a phone, the listing grid with photo carousels works, and the extensive filter panel — location with radius, services, age, physical traits, spoken languages, nationality — is usable with one thumb, though that many options means scrolling. There's no app; app-store rules keep the whole sector out. Within the Dutch field, Redlights feels more modern than Sexjobs and roughly on par with Kinky.

## How fast is the site?

A 6, with one genuine plus: at the time of this review, Redlights loaded directly, without the Cloudflare bot-check screen our test setup hit on every first visit to Kinky and Sexjobs. After that, the pages are simply on the heavy side — a lot of images loading at once — and measurements settle mid-pack. Fine on wifi, sluggish on mobile data.

## How transparent is pricing?

A 6. Redlights gives advertisers a rates field — the help centre has a dedicated page for it — and many ad texts state concrete prices: "30 min 80 euro", "150€ 60min". But cards in the listing grid show no prices, nothing is mandatory, and nobody checks whether the advertised rate matches what's asked in person. You'll build a price picture faster than on Kinky; confirming the actual number before you book remains your job.

## How good is the moderation?

The best score in this review: a 7. Every ad is reviewed by the platform before publication — within minutes to a maximum of one hour during office hours, within twelve hours outside them, after which the advertiser gets the result by e-mail. That's published in the help centre, turnaround times included.

It goes further: there's a reporting page for abuse and fraud, a separate page on human trafficking and forced prostitution, explicit rules on which photos and videos are allowed, and a distinct "safe sex" label. No other open platform in our test documents its moderation this verifiably. The caveat keeps it from an 8: pre-publication review filters on rules and imagery, not identity — a tidy fake ad built on stolen but unremarkable photos passes that kind of check.

## Which languages does Redlights support?

A 6, the best language score among the open marketplaces. The interface runs in Dutch and English, and French-speaking Belgium is served through Quartier-Rouge.be. German is missing, and most ad texts are still Dutch — so the English interface helps a visitor filter, not read the actual ad. For a tourist that's still a real step up from the Dutch-only competition.

## How big is the inventory?

A 7. Redlights covers the Netherlands and Flanders — category pages list Antwerp and Brussels alongside Amsterdam, Rotterdam and The Hague — across categories from private reception to gigolos, fetish, couples, jobs and business addresses. If you're near the border or searching in Belgium, that's a concrete advantage no purely Dutch platform offers.

But "the largest marketplace in the Netherlands", as the site's own header claims? There's no independent evidence. Investigative outlets Investico and Pointer counted an average of 14,371 daily advertisers on Sexjobs; no comparable count exists for Redlights, and our own city-by-city sampling suggests less volume than the big two. A solid inventory with unique Belgian coverage: yes. The largest: not demonstrated.

## Who is Redlights best for?

Anyone searching the Netherlands and Belgium together — no other platform in our test does both in one query — and visitors who don't read Dutch, thanks to the English interface. It also suits people who like to read the rules before relying on a platform: the help centre answers questions competitors bury in vague terms.

The flip side comes with it: the verified label is a photo check, so treat every listing as something you screen yourself — never pay in advance, distrust rates far below market, and use the reporting page when something smells off. The verified-only filter is a useful first sieve, not a guarantee.
