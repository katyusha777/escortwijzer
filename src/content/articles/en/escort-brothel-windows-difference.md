The difference is who travels and who holds the licence: an escort comes to you (usually your hotel), while at a window, club or privéhuis — the Dutch term for a small, appointment-based brothel — you go to the worker. Prices follow the same split: windows start around €50 for 15–20 minutes, independent escorts charge €150–€250 per hour.

All of it is legal. The Dutch government puts it plainly: prostitution is legal in the Netherlands as long as it involves sex between consenting adults ([government.nl](https://www.government.nl/topics/prostitution)). What differs per form is who needs a municipal licence and where you'll actually find it.

## What do the Dutch terms actually mean?

Dutch ad sites use a vocabulary that translates poorly, so here is the decoder:

- **Escort** (also "bezoek", visit): the worker travels to a location you arrange — hotel room or home. Booked by phone or chat, charged per hour. Independents handle everything themselves; agencies mediate and add a margin.
- **Privéontvangst** ("private reception"): the reverse — you travel to the worker's private address, a home or rented workspace. **Thuisontvangst** is the same thing named from the worker's side: receiving at home.
- **Raamprostitutie**: window prostitution, the famous red-lit rooms.
- **Privéhuis**: a small brothel without a bar, often appointment-based. A **club** has a bar, an entrance fee and communal choosing ([Sekswerkerfgoed](https://sekswerkerfgoed.nl/vormen-van-prostitutie-een-overzicht/)).

These are not just words. On Kinky, the biggest ad platform in the Benelux, "escort" and "privéontvangst" are separate ad categories — the market itself splits on who travels. More terms are in our [glossary](/en/reference/glossary/).

## How do the Amsterdam windows work — and are they escorts?

They are not, and mixing the two up is the most common tourist misunderstanding. A window worker rents a room from a licensed operator and works for her own account; the operator only rents out the space and has no say over her method or clients (Sekswerkerfgoed). You negotiate price, time and services directly at the window and pay cash. In Amsterdam, workers at licensed businesses — the windows of De Wallen included — must be at least 21.

The windows are also a shrinking piece of the market. Of roughly 1,500 windows nationwide in 2010, about 1,000 remain, spread over fewer than ten cities; Utrecht closed all of its windows back in 2013, and Amsterdam has spent years exploring moving Wallen windows to an "erotic centre" ([NOS, July 2025](https://nos.nl/artikel/2575211-steeds-minder-raamprostitutie-een-op-de-drie-ramen-gesloten-sinds-2010)). Most of the Dutch market now lives online, on ad platforms — for how that plays out in the capital, see our [Amsterdam escort guide](/en/cities/escort-amsterdam/).

## Who needs a licence, and where is each form legal?

Five types of sex business need a municipal operating licence: escort agencies, window businesses, brothels (clubs and privéhuizen), erotic massage parlours and sex cinemas ([Ondernemersplein](https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/wetten-en-regels/vergunning-seksbedrijf/)). The licence sits with the operator, not the worker: a window worker or club worker needs no licence of her own, and a fully independent escort who handles her own ads, contact and transport works without one in Amsterdam ([ACS](https://amsterdamcenterforsexworkers.nl/voor-sekswerkers/starten-stoppen/vergunning-nodig/)).

Home reception is the one form where cities genuinely diverge. Amsterdam issues no permits for it at all — it conflicts with zoning — so structural home reception is effectively not legal there (ACS). Rotterdam allows solo work from the worker's own registered home without a licence, under strict conditions including advertising at most once per thirteen months ([City of Rotterdam](https://www.rotterdam.nl/sekswerk)).

| Form | Who travels? | Licence needed by | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Escort (outcall) | the worker | the agency; full independents usually none | nationwide |
| Privéontvangst (incall) | you | varies by city: banned in Amsterdam, solo licence-free in Rotterdam | varies by city |
| Windows | you | the window operator | fewer than ten cities, incl. Amsterdam and The Hague |
| Club / privéhuis | you | the operator | nationwide, capped per municipality |
| Erotic massage parlour | you | the operator | nationwide |

Licensed businesses also need an approved business plan and a Bibob integrity screening, and the big cities set a minimum worker age of 21 (Ondernemersplein). ## What does each form cost?

Advertised-rate bands as of mid-2026, rounded conservatively:

- **Windows:** from about €50 for 15–20 minutes; everything beyond that is negotiated per window, in cash.
- **Privéontvangst:** typically €120–€200 per hour — usually below escort level, since the worker has no travel time.
- **Escort:** €150–€250 per hour independent, €250–€400 through an agency, plus an outcall surcharge for hotel visits.
- **Club/privéhuis:** entrance fee plus a per-time rate per worker; the total lands near escort level.

## Which form makes sense for a visitor?

Our take: if you're staying in a hotel, an outcall escort is the practical default. She comes to you, screening runs both ways, and you're not hunting for an unmarked address in an unfamiliar city. Most Amsterdam hotels allow guests on your room; some hostels and budget hotels don't, so check first. The honest downside: it's the most expensive form, and fake ads on open platforms target tourists specifically — read [how to spot fake escort profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/) before you book anything.

The windows suit a different itch: no appointment, you see exactly who you're talking to, and the price is agreed before anything happens. But 15–20 minutes is short and transactional, and on De Wallen you'll do the negotiating with a tour group walking past.

Privéontvangst is the budget pick if you can travel — just know you're a guest at an unknown address, and in Amsterdam the form operates without a licence and therefore without oversight. Clubs are for making an evening of it, with the entrance fee and bar prices as the built-in premium.

Whatever the form, the client-side law is identical: booking is legal as long as the worker is 18 or over and acting voluntarily.
