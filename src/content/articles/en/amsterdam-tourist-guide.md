Yes — as a tourist you can legally book an escort in Amsterdam, in English, from your phone, and have her come to your hotel: sex work between consenting adults is legal throughout the Netherlands, and being a foreign client changes nothing about that. Plan on €200 to €350 for an hour with an independent escort, paid in cash when she arrives.

That is the answer. The rest of this guide is the part nobody prints on the airport posters: how the booking actually goes, what your hotel will and won't tolerate, why the cash matters, and the three scams that exist purely because eighteen million visitors a year land here not knowing the local rules. Think of it as the operations manual for someone clearing passport control at Schiphol on a Friday evening with a free weekend ahead.

## Is any of this risky for you legally?

No — provided you stay inside three bright lines. The Netherlands legalised sex work nationally, and Amsterdam regulates it through municipal licensing; what remains criminal for a client is anyone under age, anyone coerced, and buying from the street, which Amsterdam's by-law bans outright (APV article 2.12). A visitor who books a verified adult through a platform or licensed agency is doing something as lawful as ordering room service.

Note what is *not* on that list: your hotel finding out, your name in a register, anything following you home. Clients are not registered anywhere. The full legal picture — including the pending national move to a minimum age of 21 — is in our explainer on [whether escorts are legal in the Netherlands](/en/law/is-escort-legal-netherlands/); the Amsterdam-specific licensing detail lives in our [Amsterdam city guide](/en/cities/escort-amsterdam/).

One clarification worth two sentences, because visitors constantly conflate them: the famous windows of De Wallen and the escort market are different industries that happen to share a city. Windows are walk-up, in-person and anything but discreet; escorts are booked online and come to you — and if the distinction between windows, clubs and escorts is still fuzzy, our [forms-of-sex-work explainer](/en/law/escort-brothel-windows-difference/) untangles it properly.

## Do you need Dutch to book?

No. English is the de facto working language of the Amsterdam escort market: the platforms where independents advertise run English interfaces, profiles are written in English as standard, and messaging in English raises zero eyebrows. Agencies answer their phones in it.

This has a corollary that will save you money: **you never need a middleman.** Anyone who offers to "arrange a girl" for you — a taxi driver, a doorman, a friendly stranger, a "booking agent" who found you on a forum — is inserting themselves into a market that has no role for them. The entire industry is built for direct contact between client and professional.

## How does the booking actually go?

Five steps, maybe twenty minutes of effort: pick a platform, filter for Amsterdam and verified profiles, read a few profiles properly, send a short message, confirm time and place. A normal first message states when (tonight, 22:00), where (your hotel, by name), for how long (one hour), and asks whether she's available — no negotiation theatre, no explicit menu discussion; services are listed on the profile.

Expect screening in return, and welcome it. A professional will ask your hotel name, room number and the name the booking is under before she confirms. That is not surveillance — it is her walking safely into a stranger's room, the mirror image of you checking her verification badge. Profiles that skip all screening and all verification are precisely the ones to avoid. On timing, here is the pattern the market follows and my honest advice with it. Friday and Saturday evenings are peak demand: the best-reviewed independents are frequently booked out by mid-evening, and what remains visible at 23:00 skews toward the market's thinnest, most scam-ridden slice. Booking 24 to 48 hours ahead — from your sofa at home, before you even fly — gets you the calmer end of the market, better choice, and time to actually verify a profile. Landing-and-scrolling works often enough that people keep doing it; it is still the worst way to meet this market for the first time. Patience is the cheapest verification tool there is.

## Will your hotel let her in?

In most cases, yes — outcall hotel visits are the standard tourist booking, and the majority of Amsterdam hotels allow registered guests to receive visitors in their rooms. Escorts who work outcall are in hotel lobbies every working day and arrive looking like any other guest; discretion is a professional skill they are better at than you are.

The honest caveats. Policies genuinely vary by property: some hostels and budget hotels ban non-guests from rooms entirely, and some places ask visitors to show ID at reception or register overnight guests, occasionally with a fee. These are house rules, not law, and they are not reliably published anywhere — so the practical move is to check the visitor policy when you book the room, or ask reception a neutral "can I have a guest visit my room this evening?" No Amsterdam receptionist will blink; this is the least shockable hospitality workforce in Europe.

If your hotel is a hard no, most independents offer incall at private apartments, typically in residential districts outside the centre — a ten-minute tram ride, and covered by the same verification rules.

## How do you pay — and what should a foreigner never pay?

Cash, in euros, handed over at the start of the appointment. That is the norm across the Dutch market: you pay the agreed amount when she arrives, and money is not mentioned again. Some professionals take a card or a Dutch payment app on the spot, but as a foreigner, assume cash — withdraw from a bank ATM (not the fee-heavy yellow convenience machines in tourist streets) before the appointment, not during it.

Now the other half, which matters more. **Any request to pay something before you have met — a deposit, a "reservation fee", "driver costs", a "verification payment" — via gift cards, paysafecard, crypto or an anonymous payment link is a scam, every single time.** The profile is fake, the photos are stolen, and the appointment does not exist; pay once and a follow-up demand arrives instead of a person. This is the most reported escort scam at the Dutch national fraud desk, [Fraudehelpdesk](https://www.fraudehelpdesk.nl/), and tourists are its favourite audience because they can't easily pursue anything after flying home. The full pattern catalogue — with the checks that catch fakes before you send a first message — is in our guide to [spotting fake escort profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/).

A narrow exception exists: established licensed agencies sometimes take a bank-transfer deposit for hotel bookings. From an agency with a verifiable track record that can be legitimate; from an independent profile you found an hour ago, it never is.

## Which scams are aimed specifically at you?

Three classics, all running on the same fuel — visitors who don't know the market:

1. **The taxi recommendation.** The driver knows "a good club, good price, very close". The destination pays him commission and the bill bears no relation to anything agreed. Decline every time; if you want a venue, choose it yourself.
2. **The street offer.** Illegal under Amsterdam's by-law and almost always the front end of a robbery or rip-off. Nobody legitimate sells sex on the street here.
3. **The bargain ad.** Amsterdam's cost structure makes a genuine €80-per-hour escort impossible — below roughly €100 per hour a legal independent cannot operate anywhere in the Netherlands. In this city a steep discount is not a deal; it is the scam announcing itself.

## What does the weekend actually cost?

Realistic numbers for one booking, consistent with our full [Dutch escort price guide](/en/guides/escort-prices-netherlands/): an hour with an independent Amsterdam escort runs **€200 to €350**, plus **€30 to €80** outcall surcharge to your hotel. Agencies start around €250 per hour with vetting included in the price. Longer is proportionally cheaper — two hours costs roughly 1.7 to 1.8 times the hourly rate, and an overnight with an independent typically lands between €800 and €1,200. Tipping is not expected in the Netherlands; paying exactly the agreed amount is completely normal, rounding up after a great evening is a kind gesture, nothing more.

So for a Friday-night, one-hour hotel booking: withdraw €250 to €400 and you are covered, surcharge included.

Conduct-wise, the rules fit in a sentence: be showered, be sober enough to be good company, have the exact amount ready, and treat the agreed services and the agreed time as the whole deal — everything beyond that is a fresh agreement, not an assumption.
