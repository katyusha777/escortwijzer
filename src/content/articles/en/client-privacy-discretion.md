Booking an escort in the Netherlands is legal, and you are on no register for it: no client list, no record, nothing. The only place a booking leaves a trace is your bank statement — and only if you pay digitally. Cash leaves nothing; a Tikkie or transfer leaves a line with a name on it.

This isn't a guide about being sneaky. It's data hygiene for a legal service: what actually gets recorded, who sees what, and how to keep your privacy without compromising the safety of the person you're meeting.

## Am I registered anywhere as a client?

No. There is no national register of clients, and none is coming. Paid sex between consenting adults has been legal since the brothel ban was lifted on 1 October 2000 — the full legal picture is in [is escort legal in the Netherlands?](/en/law/is-escort-legal-netherlands/)

The only law that ever put registration on the table, the Sex Work Regulation Act (WRS), would have registered sex workers, not clients — and the government withdrew it. As a client you only face legal risk in two situations: a sex worker under 18, or someone working under coercion. What is and isn't punishable is spelled out in [when is a client breaking the law?](/en/law/escort-legal-age-client-risk/)

One honest caveat: there's also no public register of licensed agencies, so you can rarely verify a licence yourself. That's a weakness of the system — but it works in your favour on privacy, because there's simply no central place your name could land.

## Does booking an escort show up on my bank statement?

That depends entirely on how you pay. There are only two categories, and the gap between them is large.

**Cash leaves no trace.** No name, no description, no counterparty, no middleman keeping a copy. Bring the exact amount in small notes — that's the norm anyway (see the [etiquette guide](/en/guides/escort-etiquette-first-time/)) — and there is nothing to find.

Digital payment always leaves something:

- **Tikkie or iDEAL:** a line on your statement with the name or trade name behind the payment request, plus whatever description the other person entered.
- **Bank transfer:** the recipient's name and account number, in black and white.
- **Card payment on the spot:** this too produces a statement line, showing the payment terminal's name or the trade name attached to it.

The point isn't that digital payment is "suspicious" — it's an ordinary legal payment. The point is who reads your account. A joint account, a partner who opens the banking app, an accountant scanning your card payments: all of them see that one line. Cash is the only method that rules that out completely.

## Why is paying digitally in advance doubly wrong?

Because it creates two problems at once: a scam signal and a privacy leak.

The scam part first. A legitimate independent escort does not ask for a deposit via a payment link, paysafecard, gift card or crypto "to confirm the booking". That's a scam in almost every case — it's flagged the same way in our [glossary](/en/reference/glossary/) and in the guide on [spotting fake profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/). You lose the money and nobody shows up.

The privacy part is subtler. Paying up front ties your name to the transaction before you know the person is real. If it's a scam, you've handed over not just money but your name and account details — to exactly the kind of party you least wanted to reach. Paying at the start, in cash, to someone standing in front of you reverses the order: see first, then pay, no trace.

## How do I handle communication?

With a separate channel. A second SIM, an eSIM or a separate messaging account keeps your bookings apart from your everyday identity, and that's entirely normal.

What a separate channel is not: lying during screening. Screening — your name, your number, sometimes your hotel details — protects the safety of a sex worker meeting a stranger (explained in the [etiquette guide](/en/guides/escort-etiquette-first-time/)). Using a separate number is fine; giving a fake name during screening undermines the very mechanism that keeps the other person safe. Discreet and honest can coexist.

Know who sees what, too. While you message through the platform, the platform sees those messages, your account and your IP address. Move to WhatsApp or Signal and the platform no longer sees the conversation — but your phone number now sits with the advertiser, who keeps it. There is no channel where nobody sees anything; there is only the choice of who sees what.

Finally, watch your photos. A screenshot of an ad is harmless, but a photo you take yourself — of your hotel room, your surroundings, you — can carry EXIF data: location, time, device. Don't send those. They say more about where you are than you intend.

## What do platforms and advertisers keep about me?

Two parties keep two different things. The advertiser keeps what you send them: your chat history, your phone number, whatever you shared at screening. The platform keeps your account and log data, and everything that ran through the platform.

"Verified" next to a profile tells you something about the advertiser, not about your privacy — and a verification system where a platform builds a database of ID documents is itself a risk. What verification actually checks, and why that has a downside, is in [verification explained](/en/guides/escort-verification-explained/).

And "discreet" in an ad? That's a promise about behaviour: no nameplate on the door, no awkward conversation in the lift, no messages that give you away. It is not a legal commitment about data handling. An advertiser who promises "100% discreet" has said nothing about how long your number stays in the phone.

## How do I stay discreet at a hotel or at home?

With a few practical habits that cost nothing. For an outcall at a hotel, arrive and behave like any other guest; give your room number only at final confirmation, not in the first message. Walk to the lift separately, no scene at reception. Most hotels don't care about visitors — you just need to avoid drawing attention.

If you host at home (incall at your place), give your address only at confirmation, make sure no housemates are watching, and tidy up. Discretion here is mutual: you expect your details to stay private, and the person across from you expects exactly the same — so never share their real name, face or private details, not even "anonymously" on a forum.

## So what is actually private — and what isn't?

Cash plus a separate contact channel is the only real privacy. Everything else trades privacy for convenience: a Tikkie "because it's easier", your main number "because nobody will see it anyway", card "because you had no cash on you". Each of those choices records a line or a number that otherwise wouldn't exist. If privacy matters to you, this is the cheapest insurance there is: withdraw cash before you go, and use one separate number.

The honest limitation: untraceable doesn't exist once you communicate with a human. Screening asks for a name and a number, and rightly so — it keeps the sex worker safe. You always trade a little privacy for the other person's safety, and that's how it should be. The realistic goal isn't "invisible" but "private and responsible": no digital payment trail, no data you didn't have to give, and respect for the fact that the person opposite you expects the exact same discretion from you.
