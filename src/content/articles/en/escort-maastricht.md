Hiring an escort in Maastricht is legal: sex work between consenting adults is permitted throughout the Netherlands, and Maastricht licenses escort businesses under chapter 3 of its municipal by-law, the APV (*Algemene Plaatselijke Verordening*, CVDR676542, in force since 1 January 2024). What sets the city apart is not a window district — there isn't one — but its position. Aachen in Germany and Liège in Belgium sit about 30 km away each, which makes this tri-border city the closest piece of the Netherlands for a large slice of two neighbouring countries.

There is nothing to see here. No windows, no red-light district, no street zone. The whole market runs by appointment: escorts to your hotel or apartment, independents receiving at private addresses, and a handful of licensed venues. This guide covers what the city requires, what a booking realistically costs, and why a border city calls for extra care about who you book.

## Is escort legal in Maastricht?

Yes, under municipal licensing. Chapter 3 of the APV — "sex establishments, sex shops, street prostitution etc." — governs the whole trade. Article 3.4 is the core rule: running a sex establishment or an escort business without a permit from the municipality is prohibited. An *escortbedrijf* (escort business) is defined in article 3.1 as one offering prostitution carried out somewhere other than the business premises — meaning the meeting happens at your place, not theirs.

An agency operating in Maastricht without that permit is breaking the law. The client booking through it generally is not — but you would be funding the illegal end of a legal market.

One age point clears up a common confusion. The by-law requires the *operator or manager* of a sex business to be 21 or over (article 3.5) — that is a rule for the business owner, not for the sex worker. For the workers themselves, the Maastricht by-law sets no local minimum age, so the national floor of 18 applies. We state 18, not a local 21, because the municipality has not published the latter for workers.

## Does Maastricht have a red-light district?

No, and it never really had one. The by-law addresses street and window prostitution in article 3.10, and the outcome is plain: no permit is granted for a window-prostitution establishment. Maastricht does not even appear in the national lists of window-prostitution cities — unlike Amsterdam, The Hague, or nearby Eindhoven with its single small square.

For a visitor that means one thing: there is no "Maastricht red-light district," whatever a search engine promises. Anyone who approaches you on the street or along the Maas is operating outside the law by definition. The market is entirely online.

That is the heart of the Maastricht model, and also its weak spot. With no physical place to check anything, you depend entirely on what a profile or platform shows you.

## How is the scene organised?

Three formats, all by appointment: escort, private reception, and licensed venues.

**Outcall escort.** The escort comes to your hotel or apartment; or you go to an incall address. Booked through a licensed agency or directly with an independent on an advertising platform. This is the largest segment and the standard visitor booking.

**Incall / privéontvangst.** *Privéontvangst* means "private reception" at the worker's own address — usually the cheapest option, because no agency margin or travel time is priced in. The differences between escorts, private reception and windows are laid out in our [guide to the Dutch formats](/en/law/escort-brothel-windows-difference/).

**Licensed venues.** Clubs or private houses holding an operating permit, where you meet on the spot. The city does not publish how many there are.

Because there are no windows, the online ad market *is* the whole market — and in a border city, where many visitors come from outside and miss the local context, that is a real problem.

## What does the tri-border position mean for you?

That much of the demand comes from outside the Netherlands. Aachen is about 30 km away, Liège roughly the same — for a German or Belgian visitor, Maastricht is often closer and more discreet than their own nearest city. The city already draws weekend traffic from both countries, and escort demand follows the same pattern.

Two practical effects. Ads more often run in multiple languages, because German- and French-speaking clients are routine here. And the fake ads target exactly that visitor: someone who doesn't know the city, doesn't read Dutch, and can't easily judge what a normal rate or a normal way of working looks like.

So my advice for Maastricht is sharper than for a window city: book only through a verified profile on a serious platform. In a city with windows you can at least look with your own eyes; here, verification is your only filter, and it matters more the farther you've travelled. ## How do you book safely as a visitor?

Cash at the start of the appointment, in euros; many escorts also take a Dutch payment app or a card on the spot. What nobody legitimate does is demand prepayment by payment link, gift card or crypto before you've met — that is the single most common escort scam aimed at foreign visitors, and it is exactly the trap a border city breeds. Our guide to [spotting fake escort profiles](/en/guides/spot-fake-escort-profiles/) shows how to filter it out.

Expect screening in both directions: a professional will ask your hotel name, room number and the name on the booking. That is not suspicion, it's standard practice, and a better sign than someone who asks nothing.

The honest trade-off: Maastricht keeps no public register of licensed escort businesses, so you can't independently verify a permit as a client. You depend on what the agency or platform shows you and on the escort's online footprint — a weakness of the system that weighs more here, because the whole market is online and much of it is aimed across the border.
